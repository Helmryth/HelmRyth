## Design Context

### Users
Helmryth is for founders, operators, developers, and small teams who already trust capable models with real work but refuse to surrender visibility or judgment. They use the product for long-running research, engineering, operations, communication, and recurring work across local tools, connected services, browsers, and isolated computers.

### Brand Personality
Commanding, luminous, and deliberate. Helmryth feels like a beautifully made physical instrument: calm under load, explicit about state, and quietly opinionated. It is confident without swagger, warm without mascot cuteness, and technical without terminal cosplay.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light-first "editorial instrument panel." Warm mineral paper, graphite ink, vermilion command accents, petrol status signals, fine rules, asymmetrical navigation, and a braided signal mark. Interfaces should feel composed from labeled plates, ledgers, and live work surfaces—not floating glass, dark command-center clichés, card grids, purple-blue gradients, or generic AI chat chrome.

The product is remembered for its rhythm: a narrow score rail, strong typographic hierarchy, operator sigils that behave like living instruments rather than faces, and a visible pulse line that connects work, decisions, and approvals.

### Design Principles
1. **The work is the hero.** Show artifacts, state, decisions, and next actions before decoration.
2. **Every surface has a job.** Flatten hierarchy; use rules, spacing, and typography before adding a container.
3. **Autonomy stays legible.** Running, waiting, blocked, and gated states must be unmistakable without relying on color alone.
4. **Light carries authority.** Use warm light surfaces with dense graphite type; reserve vermilion for command and petrol for live signal.
5. **No inherited tells.** No mouse/triangle mascot, Grok palette, black Telegram shell, generic bot vocabulary, glow, gradient text, glassmorphism, emoji icons, or fake metrics.
6. **Motion explains change.** Only animate transforms and opacity; respect reduced motion and never use bounce.

### Product Language
- Bots → Operators
- Groups / rooms → Crews
- Tasks / threads → Runs / workstreams
- Plugins → Capabilities
- Routines / automations → Cadences
- Approvals → Gates
- Computer → Workbench
- Team map → Operations map
- Chief of staff → Lead operator
- Inspector → Trace
- Skills → Methods

### Voice
Short, concrete, and accountable. Prefer “Rivet is waiting at a gate” over “Your AI agent requires approval.” Prefer “Start a run” over “Create a new task.” Never use “magical,” “revolutionary,” “supercharge,” “unlock,” “AI-powered,” “copilot,” or “assistant.”
